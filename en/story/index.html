About

What I have built through school and work, in order.

I played a lot of games growing up. Around the time I got tired of playing within someone else's rules, I started wondering whether making the rules myself would be more fun, so I went to a high school that teaches game development as a major. Today I implement the rules of slot games in server logic and the Unity client.

When I used what

  1. 2018 ~ 2020

    Making Games Without an Engine

    C++, DirectX 9

    In October 2018 I made a shooting game to apply to the skills team at Seoul Digitech High School. It is the oldest work I still have. After that, I recreated Cookie Run, MapleStory, and Overwatch in DirectX, writing the framework myself from scratch.

    This was also when I wrote my own imitation of the STL list and built A* pathfinding and BSP map generation. I also completed a 60-hour DirectX 9 game development course at the Sogang University Lifelong Education Center.

    On the skills team, I went from 5th place at the 2019 Regional Skills Competition to 3rd at the regional level and 5th at the National Skills Competition in 2020. That same year, I won the Grand Prize at the Employment UP! Game Development Competition for IT Vocational High Schools.

    DirectX Cookie Run cloneA* pathfindingBSP map generation

  2. 2019 ~ 2020

    Building as a team, all the way to release

    Unity, team development

    I regularly joined events like AppJam, Highthon, KOI Game Jam, and Indie Game Weekend, where three or four people had 12 hours to finish a game.

    Bright Burn, which I built over six months with Team Oh! Rock, was exhibited at G-STAR and Gamescom and released in 2020. Ttangpaseo Donnaonya?! (Dig for Money?!), made in 20 hours, came out the same year.

    Around this time I also started using Unity and learned how to share code on a team.

    See Bright BurnSee Ttangpaseo Donnaonya?!

  3. 2020 ~ 2021

    My First Game at a Company

    Unity, combat AI

    In July 2020 I joined 9M Interactive and developed a card-deck merge battle game. I worked on core combat, unit AI, crowd movement, and the out-game UI.

    I also formed a separate team to make mobile games and learned to integrate services such as PlayFab, BACKND, and Google Play Games.

    Personal project: MTalk, a TCP chat program

  4. 2021 ~ 2024

    Building Server and Client Together

    TypeScript, Unity

    I joined Bagelcode in December 2021 to develop slot games. This is when I began writing server logic in TypeScript as well as the Unity client.

    I developed 8 new slots in 2022 and 3 in 2023.

  5. 2024 ~ present

    Back to School While Working

    p5.js, product planning, business documents

    In 2024, I enrolled in the Department of Media Management at Soongsil University, and I have been balancing work and school since.

    In my first semester, I was the development lead on DangunMyth, a project based on the Dangun founding myth, and the team moved its Processing project to p5.js and put it on the web.

    DangunMyth repository

  6. 2024 ~ 2026

    Branching Into Casual, Then Back to Slots

    Unity, SDK integration, TypeScript

    In July 2024 I joined the casual mobile game Pack & Clash in progress and worked on in-game UI, features, a sound management system, and CDN patching. In January 2025 I returned to slots and developed 5 slots that year.

    In September 2025 I set up a hyper-casual mobile game project and worked on the Unity client and the ad, payment, and analytics SDK integrations. In 2026 I expanded bonuses in existing slots and developed 1 new slot.

  7. 2026 ~ present

    Widening My Scope with AI Agents

    Three.js, web, apps, AI agents

    Around July 2026 I began handing most of my work to AI. At work, I build an AI desktop app that supports slot development and an agent evaluation tool, and I quickly validate prototypes at internal game jams.

    My personal projects also tackle problems outside games: a PC build assistant, a job-prep app, a 3D roguelite. Solo CEO, which turned a class business plan into a product, is one of them.

    What I value more than speed is verification. The faster AI builds, the more I try to put evidence such as tests and run logs in place first.
