Rescue MealAI

A food inventory and meal planning assistant that tells you what to use first

In development, Service, Sep 2026 ~ Oct 2026

OpenOpen GitHub repository

Runs without a server, using sample ingredients and a sample receipt. Refreshing returns to the start.

The problem

A printed expiration date and how food has actually been stored are two different things. The app tracks them separately to keep food safe, with the goal of reducing waste.

For one- and two-person households who gave up on food tracking apps and lose track of what to eat first.

Key features

Receipt, barcode and label entry

Photos and PDF receipts become candidates, and only what you confirm enters your inventory.

What to check first

Printed dates, storage location and opened status set the order.

Meals from what you have

Plans fit your time and servings, and missing items move to the shopping list.

Storage history

Moves, opening, freezing and partial use are recorded, with custom places like a kimchi fridge.

What I did

  • Suggested meals built around the ingredients to use first

Architecture

A mobile web app and an API, with heavy OCR moved to a separate worker.

  1. Client: React, Vite PWA
  2. API: FastAPI, PaddleOCR worker, meal planning
  3. Data: PostgreSQL with SQL migrations, local SQLite adapter

Design decisions

Never judge whether food is safe to eat
A product name or photo cannot reveal the state of an individual item, so the app stops at entry, storage history and priority.
Keep printed dates and estimates apart
Packaging dates, use-by dates and estimated priority mean different things. Source and type are stored, and an estimate never becomes a use-by date.
Review receipts before stocking
OCR can misread dates, quantities and discounts, so there are draft, review and commit steps. Restaurant receipts stay out of inventory.
Track each purchase separately
Purchase dates and storage differ, and part of an item may be frozen, so product data and stock lots are separate.

Challenges and solutions

A failed save could leave a consumption record without matching inventory.
Operations like completing a meal or committing a receipt run as one unit, restore on failure and return a retryable error.
Unstable networks could send the same request twice and duplicate stock.
A shared module records retry keys so a repeated request runs once.
