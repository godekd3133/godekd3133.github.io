Career CenterAI

A job preparation app that connects everything from choosing a posting to reviewing an interview

Private beta, Service, Oct 2026

Open

Runs without a server, using a sample job posting and a script. AI responses and scores are examples, not real results.

The problem

When resumes, cover letters, and interview practice live in separate tools, it's hard to tell whether practice is paying off. I centered the app on a loop of answering the same question again to see how much you've improved.

For job seekers preparing alone, from choosing a posting to reviewing interviews, who want to know if their answers are improving.

Key features

Goals that start from a posting

Choosing a posting creates five stages: research, compare, prepare, practice and interview.

Posting versus your experience

Experience cards are matched to requirements to show gaps, and required tasks come only from what the posting actually says.

Voice mock interviews

Questions are read aloud with live captions, and follow-ups dig into the answer you just gave.

Answer the same question again

See the weak spot, answer again and compare before and after.

Speech analysis

Filler words, pauses and pace are measured and added to the report.

Aptitude practice

Personality and aptitude tests, company-style mock exams and assessment games.

What I did

  • Gap analysis comparing posting requirements against experience cards
  • Voice mock interviews with live captions and follow-up questions
  • Speech analysis based on Silero VAD
  • Delivered PWA, Electron, and Capacitor apps from a single codebase

Current status

  • I checked the questions with AI virtual personas and have not yet validated them with real users.

Architecture

Shared rules live in one package used by web, desktop and mobile. The server relays AI calls and reads job postings.

  1. Client: React PWA, Electron, Capacitor, Silero VAD
  2. API: Express, posting reader service, schema-validated AI provider layer
  3. Data: on-device IndexedDB, a sync vault that stores only ciphertext

Design decisions

Records stay on the device, sync is encrypted
Interview records are sensitive. An AES-GCM key derives from a sync code that never goes to the server, and audio recordings are not stored.
One schema for all AI providers
Shared zod schemas keep responses the same shape when the provider changes.
Growth tied to measurable actions
A character that grows just for opening the app means little, so it grows from finished tasks and improved speech metrics.

Challenges and solutions

Browser speech recognition tends to drop fillers like "um" from final results.
The app also uses the longest interim result, and server transcription when available.
Volume alone cannot tell if someone is speaking in a noisy room.
Silero VAD checks every 100ms, falling back to volume only if the model fails to load.
