Working While Going to University

A record of enrolling in the Department of Media Management at Soongsil University while working at Bagelcode, and of how coursework turned into personal projects.

(Written in 2026, looking back.)

I finished my industrial technical personnel service on December 23, 2023, and entered Soongsil University’s Department of Media Management in February 2024. I’ve stayed employed at Bagelcode while attending, and I’m also active in the game development club Gemmaru. Slot work went on, while school brought entirely different tools and documents.

The question

School history alone can’t explain why I chose a business-side major while employed, or what changed. So I checked the repositories for traces that class documents left on real product decisions.

Background and framework

I looked at the link between classes and products in three layers: documents (whose problem is this), scope (what we don’t promise), and verification (what we checked it with). My sources are public repositories, project documents, and submitted class documents.

At first, class assignments and my own projects were separate. From 2026, planning documents I submitted in class became the starting points of personal projects.

What I learned

A business plan is not a validated business. Solo CEO isn’t released yet, and Career Center’s questions were checked with AI virtual personas, not real users. That’s why each project post separates what I confirmed from what I didn’t.

What class gave someone who only wrote code was the habit of first writing down whose problem it is, and which one. Even KBO Fans’ first-day output wasn’t code but a service plan, an app plan, and a class document.

Document traces by project

I checked the documents visible in each repository.

KBO Fans keeps a class planning document and a long work log. On June 12, 2026, I rebuilt a feature-heavy presentation into 12 slides covering service overview, problem and opportunity, product structure, and implementation status. Items listed as next steps that had actually finished were corrected to completed scope.

Solo CEO starts from a business plan, yet its implementation documents separate real payments and legal review as remaining items. The plan’s promises and the current implementation aren’t written in the same sentence.

Rescue Meal’s October 2026 document opens with the service promise and states it won’t invent food safety claims or savings. It is a case where the wording actually shrank as class work was upgraded.

Career Center keeps a week-4 worksheet, a customer persona worksheet, and an open source project proposal in the same repository as the code. Its interview record uses a format that first asks only about past behavior with no product talk, then shows a description listing pros and cons.

The lead role on DangunMyth included more than code. Splitting the file structure and merging the web conversion work appear in the repository history. Class grades and assessments aren’t in the table and aren’t used as public material.

The role of documents varied by project. On KBO Fans, as the presentation neared, more work went into matching documents to the implementation. On Career Center, class worksheets and code moved days apart: the first version came on October 15, 2026, and the week-4 hypotheses and persona worksheets entered the repository on the 18th. On DangunMyth the goal was the class result, and I found no record of treating product scope in documents. So I describe the path from class documents to products as a change appearing in 2026. Lower implementation costs from AI tools may also have played a part in those two years, so I can’t credit the classes alone.

Reflection

That classes changed my habits is my interpretation. What can be confirmed as fact is the starting documents and submission records of the 2026 projects. I can’t know whether I would have written the same documents without the classes. Still, the first-day output of KBO Fans being planning documents differs from how I worked around 2022.

The counterargument is that class documents don’t replace market validation. Business plans are written to an assessment format, so believing them as they are risks treating hypotheses as facts before anything is built. Nielsen Norman Group explains that tests with a few real users can find many major usability problems. Many of my projects still have that small step ahead of them.

This post doesn't cover the burden of doing both or personal life. Its scope is the link between class documents and products.

General lessons

  • Writing down what you won’t do gives you a basis for judgment when the implementation grows.
  • Keeping submitted documents and code in one repository lets you follow what changed.
  • Documents only organize hypotheses. Checking takes users and run records.

What’s next

Around the time I built these projects, AI tools began changing how I work. That continues in First Using Codex and Claude Code. What remains is checking the assumptions in those class documents with real users.
