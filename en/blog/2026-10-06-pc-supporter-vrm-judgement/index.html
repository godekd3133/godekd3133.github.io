Worklog: Changing the PC Supporter Motherboard VRM Judgment

The day I sorted out the VRM judgment that got tangled by a branch merge, and switched from unverified coefficients to judging by CPU power tier and board tier.

This is the day I rewrote the motherboard power delivery (VRM) compatibility rule.

What I Did

  • I merged main into the feature branch and resolved the conflicts, separating the total phase count from the Vcore phase field.
  • I wrote up the remaining VRM judgment problems after the merge.
  • I raised the engine to 2.63.0 and changed the judgment rules.
  • I corrected numeric errors in the merge record.

What Blocked Me

The two branches had fixed the same logic in different directions, and the merge result mixed the two policies. “Estimated insufficient” and “no data” both came out as the same warning, and boards with no VRM specification got a warning even with a 65W-class CPU. Common combinations like a Core Ultra 125W with a B860M 7-phase board were also being flagged as insufficient.

The estimated coefficients were weak too. The 50A per phase and 0.35 weren’t values calibrated by measurement, and the meaning of the phase count differed depending on labeling conventions.

There was also a problem that arose without any conflict marker. The automatic merge produced two assignments to the same field, and one was overwriting the other’s value.

Decisions

  • A VRM shortfall shows up as power throttling under sustained heavy load, not as a build that can’t be assembled. So a blocker is issued only when the manufacturer-stated capacity is exceeded.
  • I don’t estimate wattage with unverified coefficients. I also removed the phase count from the judgment.
  • Instead, I use the chipset as the first signal of board tier. A CPU with a maximum power of 150W or more on an entry-level board gives a warning, and an unknown board tier gives info.

Comparing against 300 snapshots from October 2, 2026, warnings dropped from 53 to 28, and info dropped from 6 to 0. All of the remaining 28 are combinations of an entry-level board and a high-power CPU. Warnings and info don’t affect the verdict state, so the distribution of states stayed the same.

What’s Left

The 150W threshold and the Vcore 300A lower bound are provisional values with no measured basis, so they still need to be decided. The tier for Intel K with H810, adding chipsets such as B840, and obtaining the boards’ CPU support lists are also still open.
