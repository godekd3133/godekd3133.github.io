Worklog: Changing the PC Supporter Motherboard VRM Judgment

The day I sorted out the VRM judgment that got tangled by a branch merge, and switched from unverified coefficients to judging by CPU power tier and board tier.

This was the day I rewrote the motherboard power delivery (VRM) compatibility rule left over from the rule engine post.

Context of the day

Looking at the verdicts left after the merge, there were too many warnings, and wording and severity didn’t match. Boards without power stage data got a warning regardless of CPU, while the text said it would run without trouble. I rewrote it that day because too many warnings make it easy to miss truly risky combinations. That last sentence is my interpretation.

What I did

I merged main into the feature branch, resolved conflicts, and separated the total phase sum from the Vcore phase field. I cleaned up the remaining verdict problems after the merge, bumped the engine to 2.63.0, and corrected numeric errors in the merge record.

Where I got stuck

Two branches had changed the same logic in different directions, and the merge mixed both policies. “Estimated insufficient” and “no data” both came out as the same warning, and boards without power delivery specs warned even for 65W CPUs. Common combinations like a 125W Core Ultra on a 7-phase B860M got flagged as insufficient.

The estimated coefficients were weak too. 50A per phase and 0.35 weren’t calibrated by measurement, and phase counts meant different things depending on labeling practice. Some problems came without a conflict marker: automatic merging produced two lines assigning the same field, with one overwriting the other.

Judgment

Before

Calculate watts from phase count and estimated coefficients to decide insufficiency.

After

Compare the CPU's maximum power class with a board class read from the chipset. Blockers fire only when manufacturer-stated capacity is exceeded.

  • Insufficient power delivery shows up as power limiting under sustained load, not as being unable to assemble.
  • No estimating watts with unverified coefficients. Phase count is out of the verdict too.
  • CPU maximum power of 150W or more on an entry-level board gives a warning; an unknown board class gives info.

The remaining 28 are all entry-level boards with high-power CPUs. Warnings and info don’t affect verdict status, so the status distribution stayed the same.

Which combinations changed, and how

Result Combination
Warning kept Ryzen 9 9950X family, 9800X3D family with A620, low-output B650M, B850M
Warning kept Core Ultra K, F, 285 with H610, H810
Warning removed Core Ultra with mainstream chipset boards such as B860M
Warning removed 65W-class CPUs generally

I also fixed the undervaluing of Intel K models. Without PPT the rule calculated from 125W base power, while Danawa’s listed MTP was 159 to 250W. MTP applies only to power stage verdicts, and cooler and PSU rules keep their earlier values. That choice keeps one rule’s fix from shaking other verdicts.

The document also keeps open decisions. Core Ultra 5 245K and 250K have 159W MTP and barely pass the 150W bar. Whether to raise the bar, or lower Intel K with H810 to info, is still undecided.

Validation used the 300-item snapshot from October 2. The unchanged status distribution was expected, since warnings and info don’t affect status. So it confirms no other verdict was disturbed, and doesn’t show the new rule is right. I corrected the numbers in the merge record for the same reason: a wrong record skews the baseline for the next judgment.

In short, the day’s results were engine 2.63.0 and a document of the reasoning, including how to roll back. With many provisional values, I expected this rule to change later.

Chipset data for 902 of 904 motherboards and MTP for all 19 Intel CPUs made the new rule applicable. There are limits. Fewer warnings don’t prove fewer false alarms. I have no measured power limits from real boards, and chipset tier is a proxy. Parts remain open to debate, such as the Vcore 300A floor that classes low-end B chipset boards as entry-level.

What’s next

The 150W threshold and Vcore 300A floor are temporary values without measured grounds, still to be decided. Grades for Intel K with H810, adding chipsets like B840, and securing board CPU support lists also remain. That same week I hit a passing-but-lacking problem on the game side.
