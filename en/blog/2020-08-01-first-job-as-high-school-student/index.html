Starting My First Job as a High School Student

A record of joining 9M Interactive in my final year of high school and working on the core and unit AI of a card-deck merge battle game.

(Written in 2026, looking back.)

In July 2020, as a third-year high school student, I joined 9M Interactive as a junior client programmer on the Sheed team. I graduated from Seoul Digitech High School in March 2021 and stayed at the company until December 3, 2021. The competition and Bright Burn’s release from earlier posts fell in the same year.

Why I started

My self-introduction gave my reason for joining a company: I wanted to carry on the value of learning and growth, heading toward endless new ground, at work as well. What I wrote about a year later became this post’s conclusion.

What I tried

I developed a card-deck merge battle game.

  • Simulation core for card-deck merge battles
  • Combat unit AI and crowd movement
  • Out-game UI

Unlike the assignments I’d made alone, this was one project carried on as a team for over a year. I kept making things outside work too.

Activity Details
Jack’s Bean A five-person team with friends. Made Morbido and Corona Yellow
Separate mobile game team Learned PlayFab, Backend, and Google Play Games integration
MTalk A TCP chat program
Releases and awards Released Bright Burn and Ttang Pa Seo Don Na Oh Nya?!, won the Employment UP! Grand Prize

The five Jack’s Bean members were high school friends then working at game companies. Morbido used QuickSheet to link spreadsheets and data, which was my experience of keeping design values outside the code.

Where my judgment shifted

The biggest change in what I wrote after joining was about my career. I wrote that after pouring affection into one project for a year, my doubts about becoming a game developer turned into certainty.

The same piece said that meeting many people in the industry made me more mature as a person and a better programmer.

Before joining

I built assignment by assignment toward competitions and releases. Whether game development suited me as a career was still an open question.

After joining

Carrying one project for a year turned those doubts into certainty.

In Jack’s Bean’s Corona Yellow, I managed resources with the Addressable package and collection data with a Custom Editor and ScriptableObject. I met Addressables again when building CDN patching for Pack & Clash in 2024.

What I learned

Handling unit AI and crowd movement was my first time at a company translating a game’s combat rules into code. From February 2021 I served as an industrial technical personnel, finishing on December 23, 2023.

What came next

In December 2021 I moved to Bagelcode and began writing server logic on top of the Unity client. That continues in Taking On Both Slot Server and Client.
