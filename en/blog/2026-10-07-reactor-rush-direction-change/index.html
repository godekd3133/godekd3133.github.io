Worklog: Reactor Rush Change of Direction

A record of the day I stopped polishing the free-for-all game and swapped the default entry point for a single-player expedition.

How It Got Here

  • October 3: I built the first version under the name Spark Raid.
  • October 5: I judged the graphics, fun, presentation, and lobby all lacking, so I rebuilt it as a core-stealing game.
  • October 6: I judged the core-stealing version lacking in fun, feel, and graphics too, so I switched the default game to Spark Showdown, a 75-second 3D bounty free-for-all.

What I Did

On October 7 I first fixed the free-for-all. I added a skippable controls warm-up, a display of the opponent’s current aim direction, and a marker for the central charge cell. For the charge cell, I also fixed a defect where the lifetime handling order made it skip the next spawn cycle.

Even so, I judged the free-for-all lacking in graphics, lobby, presentation, and core, and changed direction to single-player. I replaced the default entry point wholesale with the Reactor Rush single-player expedition. The structure is to pass through three zones and defeat a boss.

What Blocked Me

The technical checks on the free-for-all side were passing. Most of the functional items on the checklist were done and I had checked in a real browser, but I still couldn’t call it fun. The sticking point here was that passing the checks and being a good game are two different things.

Decisions

  • I decided not to go back to small free-for-all fixes.
  • I decided not to use past QA passes as grounds for the new direction.
  • I didn’t delete the old files and records. I kept the Bounty and Heist sources for historical preservation, and made a new save key so the existing wallet save data isn’t touched.

What’s Left

The first verification of the new expedition is at the level of browser input and simulation. I found and fixed the character showing its back and the cropped framing in the initial lobby, and I confirmed through real input all the way to a boss victory. Multi-touch on physical devices, audio, and a human assessment of fun were not verified at this point.
