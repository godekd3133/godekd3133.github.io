Worklog: Reactor Rush Change of Direction

A record of the day I stopped polishing the free-for-all game and swapped the default entry point for a single-player expedition.

After changing the default game three times in days, on this day I moved to a single-player expedition.

10.03 Spark Raid first version10.05 Rebuilt as a core heist10.06 75-second 3D bounty duel10.07 Single-player expedition

On October 5 I judged graphics, fun, presentation, and lobby all lacking, and on October 6 I judged the core heist version lacking in fun, feel, and graphics.

Context of the day

The standard at this point was the requirements in the product document: a lobby that feels like a game, graphical finish, choice and distinction, and presentation, plus fun and return visits for people to answer. The free-for-all passed its feature checks yet was judged negatively against these. The repository’s scope document also records that evaluation as entirely negative.

What I did

On October 7 I first fixed the duel: a skippable control warm-up, showing the opponent’s current aim, and marking the central charge cell. I also fixed a charge cell defect that skipped the next spawn cycle because of lifetime processing order.

Still, I judged the duel lacking in graphics, lobby, presentation, and core, and replaced the default entry point entirely with the Reactor Rush single-player expedition: pass three zones and defeat a boss.

Where I got stuck

The duel’s technical checks were passing. Most checklist feature items were done and confirmed in a real browser, but I couldn’t call it fun. Passing checks and being a good game are different things, and that’s where I got stuck.

What checks tell you

Features work, input registers, and there are no errors.

What they don't

Whether you want to play again, whether it feels good, whether the graphics are good.

Judgment

  • Not going back to small duel fixes.
  • Not using past QA passes as grounds for the new direction.
  • Not deleting old files and records. Bounty and Heist sources stay for history, and a new save key leaves existing wallet saves untouched.

Judgment by version

Date Default game Judgment that day
10.03 First Spark Raid Starting point
10.05 Core heist game Result of a rebuild over lacking graphics, fun, presentation, and lobby
10.06 75-second 3D bounty free-for-all The heist also lacked fun, feel, and graphics
10.07 Single-player expedition The free-for-all also lacked graphics, lobby, presentation, and core

What stands out in this table is how similar the shortfalls are each time. Changing the game format left the verdict on graphics and presentation unchanged. So it may be a problem a change of genre alone couldn’t solve. This reading fits the records of continuing separate work on lobby and character framing after the move, but it doesn’t prove cause.

Not deleting old saves also had a reason. A new save key doesn’t mix with past records and allows going back if the direction is wrong. That day’s work separated preserving history from replacing the default entry.

Fixing the charge cell defect first that day is also worth noting. The lifetime order made it skip the next spawn cycle, which was a real defect, and that fix stays even if direction changes. Free-for-all improvements like the control warmup and aim direction display, however, lost their place when the default entry changed. Folding earlier work means accepting that loss. I decided not to return to small fixes, blocking that temptation first. Finding that the new expedition’s early lobby showed the character’s back and a cropped frame came from looking at the screen, and a feature check alone wouldn’t have surfaced it.

In short, the day’s results were the replaced default entry and first expedition checks, with fun evaluation still ahead.

The next judgment needs other people’s play evaluations beyond mine. Until then the new direction stays a hypothesis.

Basis for judgment and results

After changing direction, I wrote the verification scope item by item.

Item Level of evidence
Single-player default entry Real browser
First two zones cleared, evolution choice Real browser input
Three zones, weapon evolution, boss Source and input simulation, later real input
Human evaluation of graphics Unconfirmed
Fun, return visits Unconfirmed
Physical devices, multitouch, audio, performance Unconfirmed

Changing three times in three days could be read as fast judgment or as wavering without a standard. In my interpretation, both readings have a point. The choice was possible because AI made the cost of starting over low, and it isn’t a method I could carry to other projects. That the new expedition beat the free-for-all was also unconfirmed as of that day.

What’s next

The new expedition’s first verification is at the level of browser input and simulation. I found and fixed an early lobby character showing its back and a cropped framing, and confirmed a boss win with real input. Multi-touch on physical devices, audio, and human fun evaluation weren’t verified at this point. More in What I Learned Building Reactor Rush.
