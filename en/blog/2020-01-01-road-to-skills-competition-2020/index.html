The Road to the 2020 National Skills Competition

A chronological record of what I built with DirectX 9, from the shooter I made to apply to the skills team to the 2020 National Skills Competition task.

(Written in 2026, looking back.)

When I entered high school I had no idea where to begin with game development, and my self-introduction said that starting from nothing was very hard. Seoul Digitech High School’s game development skills club made games in DirectX 9 for the game category of the skills competition. The shooting game I made to apply is my oldest surviving record.

A line from that time captures my attitude: be a better me tomorrow than today.

What I tried

2018.10 Shooting game to apply to the club2018.11 Undersea Jackpot (3 weeks)2019.03 std::list by hand (4 days)2019.04 Space Of Tank (20 hours)2019.12 Under Watch (2 months)2020.03 A* pathfinding (2 days)

Each piece practiced something different. The list’s goal was to behave identically with std:: removed from std::list. Space Of Tank was built in 20 hours in competition format, with map collision judged by pixel color. On learning that DirectX image loading has a maximum size, I split the map into several and scrolled them at random. Under Watch parsed obj and mtl files by hand and used a component-based object structure.

Along the way I also completed a 60-hour DirectX 9 game programming course at Sogang University’s continuing education center and an after-school data structures class.

Where I got stuck and what I changed

The national competition task was to make a casual action game in 20 hours, and I handled everything from design to implementation. Without an engine, every timed effect means handling timers and state variables yourself. So I built Schedule, similar to Unity’s Coroutine, which queues functions and runs them in order.

using Schedule = function<bool()>;

class CScheduleAdmin {
    queue<pair<Schedule, float>> m_Schedules;
public:
    void Add(Schedule _Schedule, float _fDuraction = 0.f);
    void Delay(float _fTime);
    void Update();
};

When a function returns false or the set time passes, it moves to the next item. That greatly cut the time to build timed effects and post-processing.

What I learned

Schedule took Unity’s Coroutine as its reference, and the component structure followed Unity’s scripting structure. I was carrying engine features from game jams into a place without an engine. My self-introduction put it this way: if you keep climbing the road others have built over a long time, the time will eventually come for you to extend it yourself.

That interest in timed flow continued. After joining a company I kept proposing UniTask over Coroutine, a story told in the slot post.

What came next

In July 2020, the year of the competition, I joined my first company as a high school student.
