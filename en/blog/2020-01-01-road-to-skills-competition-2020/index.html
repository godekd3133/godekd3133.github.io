The Road to the 2020 National Skills Competition

A chronological record of what I built with DirectX 9, from the shooter I made to apply to the skills team to the 2020 National Skills Competition task.

(Written in 2026, looking back.)

I was in the game development skills team at Seoul Digitech High School. The team made games with DirectX 9 and entered the game category of the Skills Competition, and I stayed at school until 9 p.m. every night.

What I Built, in Order

  • October 2018: I made a shooter to apply to the skills team. It is the oldest record that still exists.
  • November 2018: I spent three weeks making Jackpot Under the Sea.
  • March 2019: I implemented a list myself over four days, with the goal of making it behave the same even if you removed std:: from std::list.
  • April 2019: I made Space Of Tank in 20 hours, in the format of a skills competition task. Map collision was decided by pixel color.
  • 2019: 5th place in the game category at the Regional Skills Competition.
  • December 2019: I spent two months making Under Watch, an FPS clone, implementing obj and mtl file parsing and a component-based object structure.
  • March 2020: I implemented A* pathfinding in two days.

In between, I also completed a 60-hour DirectX 9 game programming course at the Sogang University Lifelong Education Center and an after-school data structures class at my school.

The 2020 Task

The national competition task was to make a casual action game in 20 hours, and I handled everything from planning to implementation. What helped here was a Schedule I had built myself. Similar to Unity’s Coroutine, it puts functions in a queue and runs them one after another.

using Schedule = function<bool()>;

class CScheduleAdmin {
    queue<pair<Schedule, float>> m_Schedules;
public:
    void Add(Schedule _Schedule, float _fDuraction = 0.f);
    void Delay(float _fTime);
    void Update();
};

When a function returns false or the specified time has passed, it moves on to the next item. It greatly cut production time for timed effects and post-processing.

The results were 3rd place at the 2020 Regional Skills Competition and 5th place at the National Skills Competition. That same year I won the Grand Prize at the Employment UP! Game Development Competition for IT Vocational High Schools.
