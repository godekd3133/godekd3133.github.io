Worklog: Rune Breaker Eight-Area Review

A record of reviewing eight areas first without touching code, collecting about 170 findings, and applying them starting with the highest-priority groups.

Rune Breaker is a 3D top-down hack-and-slash roguelite that runs in the browser. On this day I reviewed the features across the board and started fixing the highest-priority groups. It is solo development using AI agents.

What I Did

I reviewed eight areas in parallel: combat (player), combat (enemies and world), progression, monetization and lobby, modes, progression and story, a full pass over text, real screens, and unused resources. In the review stage I only read the code, and I attached a file and line number to every finding. For the real screens, I looked at 162 captures of mobile and desktop taken with headless Chromium. There were about 200 findings, and about 170 after merging duplicates.

The first thing I wrote down in the diagnosis was that a lot of content had been built but never reached the player.

  • The 9 phase-3 boss finales and a dozen or so unique patterns never appeared in actual combat.
  • 46 sound effect samples were loaded but never played.
  • The gear drop-rate disclosure text didn’t match the actual slots and set counts.
  • On mobile, some heroes’ aim point was fixed at 7m, or the instructions said “R key.”

The same day I applied bundles A through E and 4 structural overhauls. I connected 36 phase-3 finales and unified the text that mixed up Q and E as skills versus spells. I also deleted about 530 lines of old weapon and ultimate code that had been overwritten and went unused, and 285 unused CSS rules.

What Blocked Me

  • A cleanup was committed in an intermediate state and deleted live style rules, so I restored them separately.
  • The text cleanup and the code deletion were running at the same time, which produced overlapping blocks. I put off deleting part of the old ultimate code that had other work’s changes mixed in.

Decisions

  • I didn’t delete all of the unused content. I revived 6 shared spells as single-use rune scrolls, and I kept the sound effects as UI accent sounds while removing only 25 from loading.
  • Items that need a direction decided, like the 10 duplicated modes, I split off into the structural overhaul bundle and executed only the 4 that I chose.

What’s Left

The final integrated run had zero errors, but that is on a headless environment. I couldn’t check the installed app (simulator and TestFlight) that day. Fun was not validated by this review either.
