What I Learned Building Reactor Rush

While implementing a mechanic where you catch enemy bullets and fire them back, I wrote down what automated checks can confirm and what only a person playing can tell.

Reactor Rush is a single-player 3D action expedition game built with Three.js. You pass through three zones, across floating islands and a storm power plant, and defeat a boss. Press Q or the capture button to collect incoming enemy bullets, then press again to fire them back.

Capture Rules

  • You move with the field open and collect up to 12 shots. Pressing again counterattacks, and after 1.5 seconds it fires automatically.
  • The Return Coil evolution shortens the cooldown by 3 seconds only when you counterattack with 3 or more shots collected. An empty capture or 1 to 2 shots gets no bonus.
  • Storm Eye’s shield takes only 20% damage from regular fire, and full damage from shots taken through capture or a dodge counter.

By tying the boss shield and evolution rules to capture, I built a reason to use this mechanic into the combat itself.

What I Fixed in Input

So that quick taps aren’t dropped, RogueFireIntent holds the input until the firing batch is accepted. If there’s no direction input, dodge moves you away from the enemy, and if a wall blocks it, it moves sideways. On touch, I gave capture the biggest button on the right, and turned auto-fire on by default so players can focus on their first actions. In portrait mode, only the single nearest off-screen enemy is shown at the edge. On narrow screens the crowned character overlapped the mission panel, so I set the camera distance separately for each appearance, and I checked the framing down to 320×568.

Writing Down Checks and Fun Separately

I track what I checked and how far in a table. The record of an automatic controller clearing the game in about 33 seconds is not used as evidence of a human clear time or of fun. A new-player expedition completed using only input in a real browser is recorded separately.

The items still unconfirmed in the table are:

  • A person’s assessment that the graphics are good
  • Fun and return visits
  • Multi-touch, audio, and performance on physical devices

This game still has to receive a human assessment of play.
