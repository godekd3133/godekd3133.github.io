Owning Both the Slot Server and Client

A year-by-year account of the period at Bagelcode in which I handled 14+ slot games, from server logic through the Unity client.

(Written in 2026, looking back.)

After client work at my first job, I joined Bagelcode in December 2021. My first task added features to the slot production tool, and in January 2022 I began implementing server and client for my first assigned slot. This is when I started writing server logic in TypeScript on top of the Unity client.

The question

When one person handles server and client, a single slot can finish quickly. Making the next slot easier is a separate matter. This post separates development counts from tool proposals, and traces when and how proposals took hold in the team.

Background and framework

My sources are the list of games confirmed as released, my own review write-ups, and my own messages in internal chat. I split contribution into three stages: proposal, shared adoption, and extended adoption. Without that split, work my colleagues implemented could read as mine.

Why I touched tools and structure

The review I wrote at the end of 2023 states that year’s goal: develop without schedule delays, raise slot performance and productivity, and create an environment for boldly trying more technical slots. Along with finishing each slot fast, I cared about making the next slot easier.

My scope looked like this.

Area Work
Server Game logic and simulation
Client Reel behavior, state flow, animation, presentation
Tools Editor and reel features of the slot production tool

I implemented more than 14 slots for Club Vegas Slots, Cash Billionaire, and Jackpot Madness. I mostly wrote the server logic for my slots alone and built the Unity client with colleagues.

What I tried

Friction between roles began with proposing a meeting. I kept suggesting regular meetings between the development and TA teams; they began monthly in February 2023, and I led them, wrote the notes, and prepared reviews for the next one. My review said that without them, we would have kept putting up with these inconveniences and working inefficiently.

Technical proposals took time.

2022 Proposed reel structure and bitmap font improvements2023 Work began as optimization concerns deepened2023 UniTask adoption landed2024 Applied and extended in real slots

I kept proposing UniTask from the time I joined, to write coroutine flows more simply. A colleague did the adoption; my part was proposing it and extending its use. The new reel system was also mainly developed by a colleague, while I raised the idea and kept pushing the improvement. My 2023 review said of this that a sprout from a seed means it can become a tree.

Where my judgment shifted

My goals at the start of 2024 were productivity, team technical skill, and stable slot development. I built tools for removing empty folders and deep-copying scripts to reduce time lost to repetitive work. Onboarding colleagues also became part of my work then.

What I learned

A proposal not accepted right away can still get used if a solution is ready when a similar problem resurfaces. That is what happened when items I proposed in 2022 came back as optimization issues in 2023.

Contribution scope by improvement

Splitting the improvements on my record into need, work, and my share gives this table.

Item Why it was needed My share Recorded change
UniTask Waits and releases in coroutine features were hard to check Proposal, shared examples, adoption in new slots, standards item Team decided to use it from the next slot
Move to script-first Graphs were hard to review, diff, test, and analyze with AI Refactored symbol behavior, pushed the controller move New features written as script controllers

Grand Ways No feature for resizing all reels Developed variable-size reels Applied to Fist of Zeus
Bitmap font importer Jackpot board display cost performance Proposed it, built the importer Jackpot board performance issue resolved

The table shows two starting paths. Work that began from one slot’s needs, like Grand Ways, finished quickly, while changes to the whole flow, like UniTask, took long. A feature request has a clear owner, but with structural improvement, the slot paying the cost now and the slot gaining later differ. That comparison is my interpretation.

Cross-role meetings fit the same frame. I proposed them, and from February 2023 they ran monthly, with notes carried into the next meeting. Complaints stayed on record as agenda items rather than passing as talk. I did not measure how many hours this saved, and what I wrote in my review is my own judgment.

Reflection

The records confirm that adoption took time after the proposal. Why it took so long is less clear from what I have. In my interpretation, changing shared structure within slot schedules needed a moment when the risk could be accepted, and it seems to have started applying to each new slot only after it went into the checklist.

I was careful not to overstate my role. Colleagues did the shared UniTask adoption and the main development of the new reel system. What I developed directly includes features such as Grand Ways for Fist of Zeus. There are records that performance stabilized, but I can’t publish before-and-after numbers, and I also didn’t measure them systematically. That was a weakness.

As problem, action, and change: the problem was that I couldn’t explain the effect of proposals in numbers. In later AI tool work I changed the order and built run records and evaluation tools first.

General lessons

  • Leave a proposal with a document or example, so it can be brought out when the same problem returns.
  • A rule applies without anyone having to remember it once it goes into a checklist.
  • Decide what you will compare against before starting improvement work.
  • Writing contribution as proposal, implementation, and extension keeps it from overlapping a colleague’s share.
